# Prediction Market Reality Filter

# Architecture Philosophy

Version: 2.0

---
The system exists to discover prediction market mispricing.

Anything that does not improve this objective should be questioned.

# Why This Project Exists

Most AI news systems answer:

"What happened?"

Most prediction markets answer:

"What does the crowd believe?"

Neither directly answers:

"What is the most likely reality?"

Prediction Market Reality Filter exists to bridge this gap.

The system's purpose is not to summarize news.

The system's purpose is not to predict everything.

The system's purpose is to identify situations where reality and market expectations diverge.

---

# Core Mission

Transform information into probabilistic decisions.

The platform should continuously answer:

1. What happened?
2. What evidence exists?
3. What outcome is most likely?
4. What does the market believe?
5. Is the market wrong — or are we?
6. How confident are we?
7. Were we correct?

---

# Fundamental Belief

Reality is the final source of truth.

News is not truth.

Experts are not truth.

Markets are not truth.

AI is not truth.

Only resolved outcomes determine correctness.

Every component of the system ultimately serves reality tracking.

# Design Philosophy

## Philosophy 1

Decision > Analysis

Users do not need more reports.

Users need better decisions.

Every module should contribute directly to decision quality.

---

## Philosophy 2

Probability > Narrative

Human reasoning naturally generates stories.

Stories are often persuasive.

Stories are often wrong.

The system should prioritize probabilities over narratives.

---

## Philosophy 3

Calibration > Confidence

Being confident is easy.

Being correct is difficult.

The goal is not high confidence.

The goal is calibrated confidence.

80% predictions should occur approximately 80% of the time.

---

## Philosophy 4

Feedback > Intelligence

A simple model with feedback will outperform a complex model without feedback.

The system must continuously learn from outcomes.

---

## Philosophy 5

Edge > Accuracy

Prediction markets already provide probabilities.

Generating another probability adds little value.

The system's primary value is identifying:

Mispricing

Information gaps

Market inefficiencies

Decision opportunities

---

## Philosophy 6

Simplicity > Complexity

Every service must justify its existence.

Every module must produce measurable value.

Complexity is a liability.

Prefer fewer services and clearer responsibilities.

---

## Philosophy 7

Disagreement is a hypothesis, not an edge.

When AI probability diverges from the market price, the market may be right.

A divergence is a claim to be tested, not an opportunity to be taken.

In a liquid market, the base rate is that the model is wrong, not the crowd.

Therefore a large divergence should raise suspicion before it raises conviction.

An edge becomes real only after the loop has confirmed, on resolved outcomes,

that our divergences in this kind of situation were historically correct.

The first question of every edge is not "how big" but "is it the market's error or ours?"

---

## Philosophy 8

The loop is closed, not linear.

A calibration number that does not change the next probability is a scoreboard, not feedback.

Every resolved outcome must flow back into:

how the next probability is formed,

how the next divergence is trusted,

and which edges are worth committing to.

The pipeline is a cycle whose exit feeds its entrance.

An open loop cannot learn, and a system that cannot learn has no durable edge.

---

# System Vision

The system evolves from:

News Analysis Platform

Into:

Reality-Aware Decision Intelligence Platform

Primary Output:

Actionable opportunities generated through evidence, probability, market comparison, and reality feedback.

---

# Success Criteria

The project succeeds when it consistently identifies market opportunities that outperform market consensus.

The project fails when it merely produces longer reports.
